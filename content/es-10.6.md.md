# Topic 7: Our Place — Community Issues
## Module 10: Green Skills

### Learning Outcomes
- Identify local environmental issues.
- Observe how issues affect people and nature.
- Collect simple evidence before suggesting solutions.

### 1. What Is a Community?
A community is a group of people who live, study, or work in a shared area, such as a village, neighbourhood, school, or workplace.

**ગુજરાતી સમજણ:** Community એટલે ગામ, વિસ્તાર, શાળા અથવા કાર્યસ્થળ જેવા સ્થળે સાથે રહેતા કે કામ કરતા લોકોનો સમૂહ. તેઓ પાણી, રસ્તા, બગીચા અને જાહેર જગ્યાઓ જેવા સંસાધનોનો ઉપયોગ કરે છે.

### 2. Common Community Issues
- Plastic litter and mixed waste.
- Water wastage or polluted water.
- Air pollution from traffic, dust, or burning waste.
- Unnecessary electricity use.
- Loss of trees and green spaces.
- Blocked drainage and food waste.

**ગુજરાતી સમજણ:** કચરો, પાણીનો બગાડ, હવા પ્રદૂષણ, વીજળીનો બિનજરૂરી ઉપયોગ અને વૃક્ષોની અછત સ્થાનિક પર્યાવરણીય સમસ્યાઓ બની શકે છે. દરેક વિસ્તારમાં સમસ્યા અલગ હોઈ શકે છે, તેથી નિરીક્ષણ જરૂરી છે.

### 3. Observe Before Deciding
Ask:
1. What is the problem and where does it happen?
2. When and how often does it happen?
3. Who or what is affected?
4. What evidence can be observed?
5. What may be causing it?
6. Who is responsible for the place or service?

Do not assume the cause without checking. For example, overflowing bins may result from collection timing, bin capacity, littering, or several factors together.

### 4. Observation Table

| Issue | Location | Evidence | Who/what is affected? | Cause to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plastic wrappers | Near a shop | Wrappers on footpath | Pedestrians and animals | Bin availability or disposal habits |
| Running tap | School wash area | Water flows when unused | Water resources | Faulty tap or lack of awareness |
| Lights left on | Empty classroom | Lights remain on | Energy use and budget | No shutdown routine |

These are practice examples. Learners should record actual local observations.

### 5. Classroom Activity: Community Issue Mapping
1. Work in small groups.
2. Select a safe area, such as the school campus.
3. Observe and record one or two issues.
4. Note evidence and possible effects.
5. Discuss what further information is needed.
6. Present findings to the class.

**Safety:** Do not touch hazardous waste, enter unsafe areas, photograph people without permission, or confront anyone. Report dangerous conditions to a teacher or responsible authority.

### Quick Revision
- Community issues affect shared spaces, people, and nature.
- Observe evidence before deciding on causes.
- Good solutions should respond to actual local needs.

### Assessment
1. What is a community issue?
2. Name four environmental issues.
3. Why should we observe before suggesting a solution?
4. What details belong in an observation table?
5. Describe one local issue and who it affects.
